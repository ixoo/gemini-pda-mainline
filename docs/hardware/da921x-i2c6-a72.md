# Gemini DA921x, I2C6, and Cortex-A72 boundary

This document records the durable hardware conclusions at the boundary between
MT6797 I2C6, the Gemini's external DA921x-family buck regulator, and the two
Cortex-A72 CPUs. Detailed candidate construction, raw observations, hashes, and
failed branches remain in the linked experiment records.

## Exact durable observations

One gated invocation completed 14 of 14 fixed native transfers. Both
passes over secondary address `0x69` returned:

```text
register 0x05 -> 0xd9
register 0x06 -> 0xd0
register 0x47 -> 0xc0
```

Both passes over primary address `0x68` returned:

```text
register 0xd3 -> 0x1f
register 0x5e -> 0x00
register 0xd9 -> 0x46
register 0xda -> 0x46
```

Distinct receive prefills were overwritten. Each transfer used packed length
`0x0101`, auxiliary length zero, the FIFO receive path, a completion interrupt,
and a FIFO count that changed from one to zero. AP-DMA did not run. The
diagnostic counters changed from `0,0,0,0` to `14,0,14,14`; initialization
remained `1,1`, and the temporary retry-policy change was restored. There was
no explicit reset, error reset, fatal signature, or I2C timeout.

CPU0–7 availability, the CPU8/9-offline state, DVFSP handoff, boot identity,
and USB serviceability remained stable across the invocation. This is repeated
evidence inside one invocation on one unit, not cross-boot or cross-unit
repeatability.

The corrected Stage-27 platform-state discriminator independently proves the
first mainline platform-resource boundary. Exact live identity showed the local
platform-state source bound after resolving its SPM syscon, acquiring the
PWRAP reset handle, and mapping MCUCFG and CCI. USB, T-PHY, I2C5, keyboard, and
CPU0--7 remained serviceable; CPU8/9 stayed offline. No snapshot caller,
register read or write, reset action, protected/clock/BigiDVFS call,
publication, or CPU request was present. This is resource-acquisition evidence
on one boot, not an observation of A72 platform register state.

The immediate clock-backend successor independently proves cumulative
read-free composition. Exact live identity showed both the platform-state and
clock-backend drivers bound while the same Stage-27 serviceability state and
CPU0--7 baseline remained intact. The BigiDVFS backend and physical observer
were absent, and no platform snapshot, protected clock read, clock enable,
register operation, publication, or CPU request occurred. This is one named-
device resource-acquisition result, not protected-clock or BigiDVFS state
evidence.

The immediate BigiDVFS-backend successor closes the cumulative read-free
reader composition. Exact live identity showed the platform-state, clock, and
BigiDVFS backends all bound while Stage-27 USB, T-PHY, I2C5, keyboard, and
CPU0--7 remained serviceable and CPU8/9 stayed closed. The physical observer
was absent; no platform snapshot, protected-clock read, BigiDVFS SMC, secure
call, publication, or CPU request occurred. This proves the three minimum
probe/resource contracts compose on one exact boot, not any physical A72
state value or transition.

The isolated first-reader successor independently completed exactly one
platform call with two stable samples and 26 read-only observations. Its raw
second sample was `spm=2a00005c/2a00004c/003d8008/003d80ff`,
`mp2=00010132/00010332/00010332`, `iso=00000002`, `dcm=00000000`,
`cci=c0000000/00000000/00000000`, and `pwrap=0`. By the source-defined masks,
CPU8 and CPU9 are off in the intersection of the two CPU-status words,
B-cluster external isolation is asserted, MP2 CCI requests and global change
pending are clear, PWRAP reset is deasserted, and MP2 DCM bits 6:0 are clear.
Stage-27 serviceability remained intact and every DA921x-provider,
protected-clock, BigiDVFS, secure, publication, owner, and CPU action remained
zero. Confidence is `observed` for this exact one-unit, one-boot snapshot; the
unclassified bits, cross-boot stability, composition with later readers, and
transition ownership remain open.

The provider-ready successor independently closes composition with the next
reader. Its explicit DT phandle held the composed observer deferred until the
legacy DA921x endpoint was bound. At that point one platform snapshot again
completed two stable samples and 26 observations, followed by two stable
provider samples in fixed order `0x56,0x51,0x5e,0xd9,0xda`; both returned
`7b,c1,00,46,46`. The terminal receipt proves one provider snapshot, ten
read-only I2C transfers, zero writes and retries, and zero protected-clock,
BigiDVFS, secure, provider-action, publication, owner-mutation, or CPU calls.
The platform second sample remained within the same source-defined A72-off
classification while USB, T-PHY, I2C5, keyboard, all three suppliers, and
CPU0--7 remained serviceable and CPU8/9 stayed offline. Confidence is
`observed` for this one exact boot on the named unit. It proves dependency
ordering and stable composed observation, not cross-boot stability, physical
voltage, rail ownership, a protected-clock/BigiDVFS read, or CPU admission.

The built-in identification lifecycle independently observed natural bind at
`14/8/6`, zero-transaction unbind, and one rebind reaching `28/16/12`.
DMA-start and every write-only, register-data-write, other-shape, and
other-address counter stayed zero. The primary driver link, page-2 client and
exact I2C `dummy` driver, two identity logs, read-only sysfs, and CPU/USB/console
serviceability persisted. Page-2 sysfs visibility lagged the synchronous bind
return, so the reproducible checker uses bounded read-only polling. This is one
lifecycle on one boot, not a cross-boot stability claim.

One later exact mainline runtime independently advanced the provider boundary.
After the LK-devinfo handoff passed late validation and released the existing
I2C6 access-controller edge, the legacy client emitted one valid bound record
with 14 identity reads, two registered providers, four completed provider
reads, and zero register-data writes. Both selector values were 70 and mapped

and buck 1 disabled at that observation point. I2C6 recorded 20 non-DMA

existence and observation on one boot; it does not prove physical rail voltage,

A runtime-triggered exact successor independently closed the remaining
read-only Gate-6 observation gaps. Before the trigger it retained all 20
startup transfers. One accepted token then performed two five-register samples
in the exact order `0x56,0x51,0x5e,0xd9,0xda`. Both samples were stable:
`CONTROL_A=0x7b` with `V_LOCK` bit `0x80` clear, record-only
`STATUS_B=0xc1`, `BUCKB_CONT=0x00`, and both selector bytes `0x46`. The final
ledger contained exactly 30 completed pointer/read transfers, with 24 at
primary address `0x68`, six at page-2 address `0x69`, and zero write-only,
register-data-write, foreign-address, or other-shape transfers. Sysfs returned
read-only, CPUs 8--9 remained offline, and one native reboot returned to a
changed Gemian boot. This closes transfer attribution and stable safe-prestate
only; it does not exclude a firmware writer or prove the native two-byte write
shape.

The first firmware-writer discriminator then sampled the SCP configuration
window before I2C6 admission. Reset control stayed `0x00000000` and debug PC
stayed `0xfffffffe` across two samples 10--11 ms apart. Every AP-visible
Device-APC AO permission and master-domain word read zero twice and its control
read `0x00000001` twice. The frozen compound contract required debug PC zero
and a domain-1 permission decode of three, so it faulted before the inherited
stopped-DVFSP validation and before any I2C6 client or transfer. Public vendor
source defines reset-control zero as asserted and one as SCP release/start;
it identifies the debug-PC register but does not establish a zero reset value.
The all-zero AO view is stable but is not treated as an observation of secure
ATF policy. This is durable pre-handoff reset evidence, not yet the combined
transaction-window firmware-writer exclusion required by B1.

The corrected successor then completed that combined boundary in one exact
boot. It retained reset control `0x00000000` in both pre-handoff samples while
keeping debug PC and Device-APC record-only, completed stopped-DVFSP late
validation with zero faults, and admitted the exact 20 read-only provider
transactions. Reset control remained `0x00000000` at every transaction entry
and exit. The ledger matched the expected 14 identity and four provider reads
within the complete 20-entry startup sequence, with zero overflow, failed,
write-shaped, foreign-address, or other transfers. CPUs 8--9 remained offline,
serviceability held, and the bounded collector returned natively to changed-

named unit and exact revision; it does not prove the native two-byte write

## Observation and inference boundary

The owner later reported that some white/grey-screen cycles followed by an
apparent automatic reboot may have been reboot-path or boot-selection issues.
Those visual observations and returns to Gemian remain part of the experiment
chronology, but without an exact runtime identity, durable stage record, or
attributable crash/reset evidence they are inconclusive about whether the
candidate kernel failed. The positive identity-gated results below remain
direct observations; contrasts that depended only on an earlier visual/reboot
cycle do not establish a kernel-causal failure boundary.

The directly observed bytes, transaction shape, and preserved serviceability
support a high-confidence inference that this board uses the legacy
DA9213/DA9214/DA9215 control contract. The independent Gemian and binary
evidence specifically favors the documented DA9214 board design, but the
read-only tuple is not a unique silicon identifier.

The initial diagnostic requested pointer bytes only. It did not issue a register-data
write, change `PAGE_CON`, register a regulator provider, select a voltage,
enable a rail, or request an A72. It is therefore accurate to say that the
diagnostic requested no voltage or enable action. It is not accurate to claim
that the rail was independently measured unchanged. The later Gate-6 runtime
did issue one attributed same-value register-data write, but likewise did not
independently measure rail voltage.

Independent serviceability discriminators also establish that generic I2C
client creation, the fixed resource contract, the compatible-derived client
name, and the I2C modalias are independently serviceable in their bounded
tests. They do not identify the cause of an ambiguous earlier reboot. A
name-only `da9214-legacy` client remained
unbound and serviceable with no OF node and zero I2C activity. An unmatched
compatible OF client also remained unbound and serviceable. The remaining
software integration boundary is transport of the completed real-compatible
I2C device event. Bounded runtime classification proved that the event contains
eight ordered fixed entries, including the OF modalias, followed by one final
`SEQNUM`; the I2C fallback modalias is absent. A corrected validator reached
final stage 17 and normal cleanup with transport suppressed, while the unbound
client, USB serviceability, CPU0--7, handoff, and every zero-I2C-activity gate
passed. The next selected boot reached stage 18 only after the normal allocator
produced the exact 293-byte skb and its header, environment, credentials,
destination group, and port ID all matched; the skb was consumed before socket
traversal or multicast and the same baseline passed. This does not identify new
hardware behavior. The following selected boot retained that exact baseline,
reached stage 19 after normal mutex-protected socket-list traversal, and
observed one socket entry with zero group-1 listeners while still consuming the
skb before multicast. The count is a bounded observation of that initramfs
boot, not a general property of Linux userspace.

The read-only board-contract gate is closed. The following are not established:

- general I2C6 correctness across arbitrary messages, failures, reset recovery,
  suspend, or stress;
- a general mainline page-selector/register-data write contract: the exact
  one-message same-value write/readback shape passed once, but page-window
  changes, arbitrary registers or values, failures, and active rail
  transitions remain unproved;
- which buck output supplies the A72 cluster and who currently owns it;
- cross-boot selector/enable stability, current limits, constraints, IRQ
  behavior, rollback, or resume behavior;
- repeatable provider registration across cold boots or another unit;
- a bounded failure/rollback transaction at or beyond external-isolation clear;
- a locked membership/provider ledger, early hotplug and suspend admission,
  notifier exclusion, and bounded independent CPU9-off/last-user readbacks;
- a mainline representation of the observed SRAM-LDO, SPM, clock, CCI,
  coherency, regulator, and firmware sequence;
- longer or sustained CPU8/CPU9 execution, safe CPU8/CPU9 offlining,
  cpufreq/OPP, idle, suspend, thermal, or general scheduling policy.

## Implementation decision

Do not make the upstream DA9211/A-family driver emulate the legacy vendor ABI.
The observed chip family, register protocol, transport contract, and resource
model require either:

1. a dedicated DA9213/DA9214/DA9215 legacy-family driver and binding; or
2. a strictly separated legacy variant in an appropriate family driver, with
   no shared probe path that performs A-family identification writes.

The production implementation must retain fixed direct addresses and a
fail-closed read-only match. The completed default-off experiment does not
authorize `PAGE_CON` changes, a writable consumer path, an active rail
transition, or an A72 request.

The first-stage contract from the
[legacy-family driver design](../../experiments/2026-07-29-da921x-legacy-driver-contract/DESIGN.md)
is implemented locally. Its exact 14-transfer probe, zero-transaction unbind,
and read-only rebind have runtime evidence; a later isolated mainline profile
also registered two read-only providers and completed four bounded provider
reads with zero register-data writes. Runtime-triggered successors then
attributed all reads, observed the complete stable safe prestate, and closed
the same-boot firmware-writer transaction window with zero write-shaped
transfers. One later exact default-off experiment establishes only the reviewed
native same-value write/readback shape. It does not establish shutdown,
suspend/resume, changed-value/rail ownership, active rollback, or a production
writable consumer.

## Safety invariants

The authoritative ordered sequence is the
[roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#ordered-gates). This hardware boundary constrains every
step without prescribing their order:

- profiles rejected by the active profile-series audit are not valid
  foundations;
- require fixed-address, fail-closed matching and zero probe-time
  register-data writes;
- keep all regulator consumers and CPUs 8–9 disconnected until their explicit
  gates;
- preserve console, keyboard, USB, CPU0–7, DVFSP, I2C5/AP-DMA ownership, and
  reboot at every hardware step;
- no further write shape, changed-value transition, writable consumer, or rail
  action is permitted without its own ownership, readback, rollback, and
  resume contract; and
- treat CPU8, CPU9, coherency, OPP/cpufreq, idle, suspend, thermal, and
  scheduler policy as separate acceptance gates.

Any failure before a trustworthy register observation remains an I2C,
ownership, or serviceability failure—not regulator or A72 evidence.
