`0x80`; BUCKB changes `0 -> 1` on each complete up and `1 -> 0` on each
complete down. Every secure snapshot has complete validity and a stable
sentinel. Every clock snapshot succeeds with the immediate semaphore result.

One cross-CPU append-order inversion exists: sequence 3703, emitted by CPU8 for
`SECONDARY_ONLINE`, precedes sequence 3704 in the ring, while their timestamps
show the CPU0 `IDVFS_ENABLE` record occurred 231 ns earlier. Ordering analysis
therefore uses monotonic timestamps with sequence as a tie-breaker. All five
complete up transactions retain iDVFS enable before secondary-online by that
clock.

The last-A72-offline boundary saw BUCKB VSEL `0x32` twice and `0x3a` four
times; the subsequent disable snapshots consistently show VSEL `0x46`. These
are direct observations from the exact retained transactions, but their policy
or workload attribution is not established by this attempt.

The exact two-worker pulse is permanently skipped for this boot. Repeating the
same image with the same late userspace retrieval path would not add a durable
observation boundary. The next observer revision should latch and stop writing
after its first complete natural CPU8 up/down pair, or export that pair through
an equally early independent path, so later serviceability cannot overwrite or
obscure the initial attributable transaction.
