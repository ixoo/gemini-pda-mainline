Runtime-to-source ordering
--------------------------

The exact retained fault path was:

  hps_algo_do_cluster_action -> cpu_down -> _cpu_down ->
  __cpu_notify(CPU_DOWN_PREPARE) -> _mt_cpufreq_cpu_CB ->
  cpuhvfs_notify_cluster_off -> translation fault

The current platform `cpu_psci_cpu_disable()` rejection is called later from
`take_cpu_down()` through `__cpu_disable()`. It cannot protect notifier entry.
The panic therefore does not contradict CPU8 startup success; it proves that
the veto is at the wrong ownership layer for generic hotplug ordering.

Chosen boundary
---------------

- Clamp the HPS target for exact CPU bounds 8..9 to one while CPU8 is online,
  before its power-down loop and before `cpu_down()`.
- Independently reject CPU8/9 at the public `cpu_down()` entry before map/hotplug
  locks, profiling, and notifier dispatch.
- Preserve the existing late platform reject as defense in depth.
- Add only two synchronous CPU8 IPIs after successful secondary completion,
  at one and six seconds, with exact CPU/online/hit-count checks.

Rejected alternatives
---------------------

- Platform-disable-only: already proved too late.
- HPS-only skip: leaves another caller able to enter unsafe notifiers.
- Generic-only veto: safe but leaves HPS repeatedly targeting an impossible
  cluster count and does not express the experiment's held-online policy.
- Disabling all CPU hotplug globally: changes CPU0..7 policy and is broader than
  the observed fault.
- CPU_OFF or guessed A72 power-down: violates the accepted one-way boundary.
- Marker-only repeat: adds no independent execution/coherency evidence.

Decision
--------

Generate three logical experiment-only patches with deterministic source-drift
guards and mutation tests. The exact parent startup code and watchdog ownership
must remain byte-identical. Buildbox is the only permitted kernel build backend.
