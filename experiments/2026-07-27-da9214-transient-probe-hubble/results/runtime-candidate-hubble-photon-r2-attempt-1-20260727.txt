Interpretation
--------------

Observation: every userspace receive byte returned exactly its own distinct
prefill. The same register was tested twice with different prefills, and its
post byte followed the prefill rather than forming a stable register tuple.
The six successful ioctl returns and six transfer/DMA/start/IRQ increments
therefore do not establish that received data overwrote the CPU-visible
buffer.

Inference: Cassini's earlier 00 00 00 tuple was its zero initialization, not
evidence of DA9214 register contents. The fault is now localized to the
mainline I2C6 WRRD receive-data path or its observation/copyback boundary, but
this result alone does not distinguish wire-level receive behavior, DMA
destination programming, DMA completion/coherency, or later copyback.
Exact source inspection further narrows the counters: `dma_starts` increments
before the APDMA enable write, and the IRQ counter records the controller
interrupt rather than an independently validated APDMA completion. Neither
counter proves receive DMA wrote its destination.

Conclusion and next decision
----------------------------

Hubble booted the exact Cassini serviceability boundary and proved that
userspace probes can be transferred and run without rebuilding or rebooting
the kernel. Do not repeat Photon r2. Keep this Hubble boot alive and use the
next bounded volatile observation to localize the WRRD receive path before any
DA9214 identity, regulator-provider, or A72 operation.
